Our History & Culture


SOCCSKSARGEN’s history traces a path from early indigenous communities and powerful sultanates to its modern identity as Region XII shaped by several administrative reorganizations.


SOCCSKSARGEN developed from ancient indigenous societies, including the creators of the Maitum Anthropomorphic Pottery, into a region influenced by the Maguindanao and Buayan sultanates beginning in the 14th century. It became an official administrative region in 1975 under Presidential Decree 742, though its composition shifted repeatedly due to the creation of the ARMM and later laws that reassigned provinces. In 2001, Executive Order 36 reorganized Region XII and formally adopted the name SOCCSKSARGEN, followed by EO 304 in 2004 designating Koronadal as the regional center. The region’s boundaries changed again in 2019 when Cotabato City and 63 barangays joined BARMM, shaping the present‑day configuration of Region XII.



Cultural Highlights

Festival

  • T’nalak Festival: Held every July in Koronadal City, South Cotabato, this signature festival honors the T'nalak fabric woven by the T’boli people. It serves as a celebration of the province's founding anniversary and showcases grand tribal street dancing.
  • Tuna Festival: Celebrated in September, this event pays homage to General Santos City's status as the Tuna Capital of the Philippines. The festival is known for its culinary competitions, multi-colored ocean-themed street dancing, and the famous tuna float parade.
  • Talakudong Festival: Celebrated in September in Tacurong City, Sultan Kudarat. The word Kudong refers to a traditional, hand-carved headgear worn by the early settlers, which features prominently in the colorful street-dancing choreography.
  • Kalimudan Festival: An annual tribal gathering held in November in Isulan, Sultan Kudarat. Derived from the Maguindanaoan word for "gathering," it brings together different indigenous, Moro, and Christian groups to celebrate a bountiful harvest.
  • Sarangani Bay (SarBay) Festival: Held every May, this event is recognized as the biggest beach party in the southern Philippines. It promotes ecotourism, environmental awareness, and beach sports along the coastlines of Glan, Sarangani.

Traditional crafts & Food

  • Traditional food
    T’nalak Weaving (T’boli) — Hand‑woven abaca cloth created by “dreamweavers,” considered the region’s most iconic cultural symbol.
    Tabih Weaving (Blaan) — Another sacred dream‑inspired textile tradition.
    Brass Casting (T’boli & Blaan) — Lost‑wax method producing gongs, boxes, and ceremonial items.
    Banig Mat Weaving (Maguindanaon) — Pandan‑leaf mats with geometric patterns, widely used in daily and ceremonial life.
  • Handicrafts Fresh Tuna — General Santos City’s signature product; central to regional identity.
    Tuna Sinuglaw — Fresh tuna + grilled pork in citrus vinegar.
    Beef Kulma — Coconut‑milk curry with spices; a key Mindanaoan dish.
    Chicken Piaparan — Coconut‑milk chicken with turmeric and herbs.
    Satti — Spicy grilled meat skewers with peanut sauce.
